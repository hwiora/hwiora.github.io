About

I'm a Ph.D. candidate at the Institute of Neuroinformatics, ETH Zurich, advised by Richard Hahnloser, graduating in July 2027. I work on speech and audio machine learning.

As a research intern on NAVER Cloud's Voice Synthesis team, I developed Redwing, a watermark for speech language models that stays detectable after repeated neural-codec resynthesis. I'm now working on nonverbal vocalization generation in speech models. In my Ph.D., I fine-tuned Whisper for audio segmentation, built a clustering workflow that organized about 100M vocalizations from 80,000 hours of recordings, and developed Trajectory Variance, a label-free measure of vocal change built on a conditional generative model.

I trained in computer science and statistics at Korea University and in computational neuroscience at the University of Zurich.

Selected Research

Speech watermarking, audio foundation models, and learning from large-scale audio.

Redwing: Watermarking Generated Speech That Survives Codec Resynthesis

“Tokens Change, Structure Endures: Spectral Watermarking for Generated Speech.” arXiv, 2026. Under review at ICLR 2027. Work done at NAVER Cloud.

Speech language models generate codec tokens, and re-encoding the audio with a neural codec replaces many of them, which erases token-level watermarks. Redwing models these token substitutions as a graph and uses constrained optimization to design the watermark embedding and detector together. It is applied through logit biasing at generation time, without retraining the model or the codec.

On Moshi, 80.7% of outputs are still detected after eight Mimi resynthesis passes at a 1% false-positive rate, versus under 10% for token-level baselines. Rebuilt for the CosyVoice3 and MOSS-TTS codecs, it reaches 84.7% and 99.8%, versus 9.2% and 6.2% for KGW.

Trajectory Variance: A Label-Free Measure of Vocal Change

“Trajectory Variance: An Unsupervised Measure of Developmental Vocal Plasticity in Birdsong.” Interspeech 2026, oral (sole author).

An age-conditioned generative model, trained with optimal-transport pairing on about 680K zebra finch vocalizations, predicts how each vocalization would change at other ages. Trajectory Variance, the spread of these predicted shifts in latent space, scores developmental change without labels. It separated learned song from innate calls where nonparametric baselines did not.

Human-in-the-Loop Clustering at Scale

“Benchmarking Automated and Semi-Automated Vocal Clustering Methods.” Applied Sciences, 2026.

An embedding-based workflow in which annotators define clusters and review outliers instead of labeling every item, so human effort scales with acoustic diversity rather than dataset size. On a 1,454-vocalization benchmark, its clusters matched the experts nearly as well as the experts matched each other (ARI 0.73 vs. 0.78).

With WhisperSeg, it clustered about 100M vocalizations from 80,000 hours of recordings with about 200 hours of human work.

WhisperSeg: Whisper for Voice Activity Detection in Human and Animal Audio

“Positive Transfer of the Whisper Speech Transformer to Human and Animal Voice Activity Detection.” ICASSP, 2024. Second author.

WhisperSeg fine-tunes Whisper to output onset, offset, and vocalization-type tokens directly from spectrograms, across human and animal recordings.

I contributed dataset processing, baseline evaluation, and cross-domain analysis.
